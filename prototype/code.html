What are the typical radiological findings for a pleural effusion?

smart_toy

Radiological findings for a pleural effusion vary depending on the imaging modality used. However, general indicators across common methods include:

  • Chest X-ray (CXR): Blunting of the costophrenic angle is the classic sign, often visible when fluid exceeds 200-300 ml. A "meniscus sign" (a concave upward curve of the fluid level) is frequently seen.
  • Ultrasound: Highly sensitive. Fluid appears anechoic (black) or hypoechoic. Useful for identifying septations or loculations.
